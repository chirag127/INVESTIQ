## 1. The Core Equation: Nominal vs Real Purchasing Power

In India, evaluating investment returns in purely nominal rupees is an optical illusion. If a ten-year Fixed Deposit yields 7.0% per annum, but your effective marginal income tax rate is 30% plus cess (31.2%), your post-tax nominal yield drops to:

$$r_{\text{post-tax}} = 7.0\% \times (1 - 0.312) = 4.816\%$$

With urban consumer price index inflation running at 5.5% to 6.5%, your real wealth is contracting every single year in purchasing power terms:

$$r_{\text{real}} = \frac{1 + r_{\text{nominal}}}{1 + i_{\text{inflation}}} - 1 \approx 4.816\% - 6.0\% = -1.184\%$$

To compound wealth sustainably in India, every rupee of long-term capital must clear three consecutive hurdles:
1. Expense ratio and fee drag.
2. Direct capital gains taxation.
3. Systemic inflation.

Over the past three decades, Indian asset classes have exhibited the following long-term annualized characteristics (approximate historical CAGR):

| Asset Class | Nominal CAGR | Tax Treatment (FY 2026-27) | Real Post-Tax Return | Liquidity Profile |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Tier-1 Prime Real Estate** | ~8.0% – 10.0% | 12.5% LTCG without indexation | ~1.5% – 3.0% | Extreme illiquidity, 6–8% stamp duty friction |

## 3. Arjun's Case File: The Inflation Awakening

::: casefile-arjun
### The Situation
Arjun held ₹1,00,000 in a standard savings bank account earning 2.7% interest, believing it was "completely safe and risk-free."
### The Math
At 6% inflation, the purchasing power of that ₹1,00,000 degrades to approximately ₹55,800 in ten years.
### The Strategic Action
Arjun maintains an emergency liquidity buffer of three months' expenses in an arbitrage fund or sweep deposit, but systematically channels all long-term surplus (money not needed for 5+ years) into low-cost, broad-market index mutual funds.
:::
