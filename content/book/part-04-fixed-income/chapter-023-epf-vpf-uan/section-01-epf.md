## 1. Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) Architecture

The **Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952** mandates retirement savings for formal private sector employees in establishments with 20 or more employees:
- **Employee Contribution:** 12% of "Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance (DA)" deducted monthly from salary.
- **Employer Contribution (12%):**
  - **3.67%** allocated to the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF).
  - **8.33%** (capped at ₹15,000 statutory wage ceiling, i.e., ₹1,250/month) diverted into the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS-95).
  - Remainder above wage ceiling diverted back to EPF.

---

## 2. The ₹2.50 Lakh Annual Contribution Taxation Threshold

Historically, EPF enjoyed complete Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) status. Under the Finance Act, 2021 amendments:
- **The Limit:** If an employee's annual contribution (EPF + Voluntary Provident Fund [VPF]) exceeds **₹2,50,000** in a financial year (or ₹5,00,000 where the employer does not contribute, e.g. government employees):
- **Bifurcation of Accounts:** The EPFO splits your PF passbook into a **Taxable Component** and a **Non-Taxable Component**.
- **Taxation:** Annual interest accrued on employee contributions exceeding ₹2,50,000 is fully taxable year after year as "Income from Other Sources" at your applicable slab rate.
- **TDS Deduction:** Under Section 194A, EPFO deducts TDS at 10% (or 20% if PAN is not linked) on taxable PF interest credited.

---

## 3. Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF) Strategy

Employees can voluntarily contribute up to 100% of their Basic Salary and DA to VPF:
- Earns the same sovereign-declared interest rate as EPF (typically 8.15% – 8.25%).
- Fully backed by the Government of India.
- *Optimization Rule:* Contribute to VPF only up to the ₹2,50,000 annual combined employee limit to preserve 100% tax-free EEE compounding. Beyond ₹2,50,000, post-tax yields drop significantly for high-income earners, making arbitrage funds or equity index funds superior capital allocation choices.

---

## 4. Universal Account Number (UAN) and Transfer Protocol

When switching employers:
- Never withdraw your EPF balance if you have less than 5 years of continuous service, as premature withdrawals trigger retroactive taxation of all past employer contributions and interest deductions.
- Always execute an online **EPF Transfer (Form 13)** using the EPFO Unified Member Portal. Your Universal Account Number (UAN) remains permanent throughout your career; only the Member ID changes with each employer.
