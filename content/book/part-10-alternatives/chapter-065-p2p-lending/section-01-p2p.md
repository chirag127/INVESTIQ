## 1. The P2P Lending Illusion: Marketing vs Actuarial Risk

Peer-to-Peer (P2P) lending platforms in India heavily market "guaranteed 10%–12% returns," portraying themselves as modern replacements for bank Fixed Deposits. In financial reality, P2P lending is **unsecured subprime consumer debt** with extreme default risk, non-existent secondary liquidity, and total equity subordination.

---

## 2. RBI's Stringent NBFC-P2P Regulatory Directives (2024–2026)

Following rampant mis-selling and liquidity guarantees by platforms, the Reserve Bank of India enacted decisive regulatory interventions under the **Master Direction - Non-Banking Financial Company - Peer to Peer Lending Platform (Reserve Bank) Directions**:

1. **Ban on Credit Enhancement & Capital Protection:** P2P platforms are strictly prohibited from providing any balance sheet guarantee, first-loss default guarantee (FLDG), or assured return schemes. The investor bears 100% of the default risk.
2. **Ban on Liquidity Guarantees & Instant Withdrawals:** Platforms cannot offer "anytime withdrawal" or auto-reinvestment pools that mimic liquid mutual funds. Lenders must wait for individual borrowers to actually repay their monthly EMIs.
3. **Lender Exposure Caps:** 
   - An individual investor's aggregate exposure across **all P2P platforms in India is capped at ₹50,00,000 (Fifty Lakh Rupees)**.
   - Any investment exceeding ₹10 Lakhs mandates certification of net worth by a practicing Chartered Accountant.
   - Exposure to a single borrower across all platforms cannot exceed **₹50,000**.
4. **Mandatory Escrow Accounts:** Funds must route through an independent bank-managed escrow mechanism (one for borrower payouts, one for lender collections).

---

## 3. Tax and Loss Asymmetry in India

Under the Income-tax Act:
- P2P interest earnings are categorized as "Income from Other Sources" and taxed at your **highest marginal slab rate (up to 30% + cess)**.
- **The Capital Loss Trap:** If a borrower defaults on principal, the Indian tax code does **not** permit you to set off that bad debt loss against your other income (such as salary or capital gains). You pay full tax on winning loans while absorbing 100% of principal defaults.

## 4. Arjun's Case File: The P2P Stress Test

::: casefile-arjun
### The Pitch
Arjun was tempted to allocate ₹2,00,000 of his emergency fund to a P2P platform advertising "11.5% fixed annualized returns."
### The Risk Audit
1. If 6% of subprime borrowers default during a macroeconomic downturn, gross yields drop to 5.5%.
2. Platform commission of 1.5% reduces yield to 4.0%.
3. Full income tax (30%) on gross interest further compresses net real return into negative territory.
4. Liquidity freeze: Capital is locked for 12–36 months with zero emergency liquidation option.
### The Conclusion
Arjun rejects the allocation. Unsecured consumer credit risk has zero place in a defensive liquidity portfolio.
:::
