## 1. Succession Frameworks in India

In India, succession and inheritance are governed by personal laws determined by religion:
1. **The Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (Amended 2005):** Applies to Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, and Sikhs.
2. **Indian Succession Act, 1925:** Applies to Christians, Parsis, Jews, and marriages solemnized under the Special Marriage Act, 1954.
3. **Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937:** Governs testamentary and intestate succession for Muslims, limiting will bequests to one-third of the net estate without consent of heirs.

## 2. Ancestral Property vs Self-Acquired Property

A pervasive misunderstanding among young Indian professionals is assuming an automatic legal entitlement to their parents' property. Under the Hindu Succession Act:

- **Self-Acquired Property:** Any asset earned by an individual through their own employment, business, or gifts/wills from parents. The owner has **100% absolute testamentary freedom** to sell, mortgage, gift, or bequeath the property to anyone, completely excluding children or spouses. A living child has **zero enforceable right or claim** over a living parent's self-acquired property.
- **Ancestral Property (Mitakshara Coparcenary):** Property inherited undivided through four generations of male lineage (father, grandfather, great-grandfather). A child acquires a coparcenary interest by birth. Following the landmark 2005 amendment and the Supreme Court ruling in *Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020)*, daughters have identical coparcenary rights by birth as sons.

## 3. The Distinction: Nominee vs Legal Heir

A nominee is legally a mere **trustee/custodian** appointed to receive proceeds from banks, mutual funds, or insurance companies upon death. The nominee does **not** become the beneficial owner of the assets. Beneficial ownership passes strictly according to the deceased's legal Will, or by the laws of intestate succession if no valid Will exists.

Always execute a clear, registered Will and keep nominations synchronized across all bank accounts, mutual funds, and demat portfolios.
